For a long time, the digital signature was the end of the line. You created a document, sent it to a counterparty, waited for them to sign, and then—once both signatures were securely locked into the file—the platform considered the workflow complete. 

But when we sat down with early users and design partners, we realized something obvious: **in the real world, a signature is rarely the end. It is almost always a trigger for a business action.**

A contractor signs a final deliverable sign-off so they can get paid. A developer signs a milestone document to unlock a DAO treasury grant. A vendor signs an onboarding form to trigger their initial retainer deposit. 

Yet, under traditional platforms, these two systems—the agreement and the payment—live in completely separate universes. You sign in one tab, generate a invoice in a second, open your multisig in a third, and copy-paste transaction hashes into a spreadsheet in a fourth. 

At **Filosign**, we decided to build a bridge across that gap.

Last year, we took Filosign to **Devconnect Buenos Aires** for a showcase at DePIN Day. At the time, we were presenting our post-quantum key architecture: end-to-end browser encryption using ML-KEM and Dilithium signatures anchored to smart contracts. 

The technical crowd loved the mathematics. But when we spoke with DAO operators and web3 project leads, they kept asking the same practical question:

They challenged us to narrow our focus to a scenario where cryptographic proof was not just a nice-to-have compliance item, but an operational necessity.

That was the spark for **Programmable Settlement**.

## Designing a Non-Custodial Bridge

The challenge with combining payments and signing is that we did not want to become a bank, an escrow custodian, or a regulated money transmitter. We had no desire to touch customer funds, run pool accounts, or custody private keys. 

To solve this, we designed a **non-custodial pull-payment architecture** directly on the Base network:

1. **Rule Registration**: The sender uploads the agreement and registers a release rule on our FSPaymentValidator contract, identifying the payee wallet and required signer addresses.
2. **Token Approval**: The sender grants a limited allowance of USDC from their wallet directly to the contract (never to Filosign).
3. **Cryptographic Signing**: The recipient signs the document client-side in their browser, generating the on-chain signature.
4. **Pull Payout**: The moment the signing conditions are cryptographically validated by the smart contract, the contract pulls the pre-approved USDC directly from the sender's wallet and routes it to the payee.

```typescript
// Under the hood: The contract validates signatures before releasing approved funds
function executePayout(
    bytes32 envelopeId,
    address payer,
    address payee,
    uint256 amount,
    bytes[] calldata signatures
) external {
    // 1. Validate that the signatures match the registered rule
    require(verifySignatures(envelopeId, signatures), "Invalid signatures");
    
    // 2. Transfer the pre-approved USDC directly from payer to payee
    require(
        usdcToken.transferFrom(payer, payee, amount),
        "Token transfer failed"
    );
    
    emit PayoutExecuted(envelopeId, payer, payee, amount);
}
```

By decoupling the relay (which pays gas) from the wallet authorization, anyone can submit the transaction once signatures are complete, but no one can touch the funds without the mathematical proof of the signed document.

## Making Crypto UX Invisible

In web3, there is a recurring trap of making the user do all the work. If you force a business customer to manage native gas tokens, download browser extensions, and sign raw hex blocks just to approve a contract, they will go back to DocuSign in a heartbeat.

We wanted to preserve the sovereign benefits of on-chain agreements while hiding the complexity:
* **Google/Social Auth**: Senders and recipients can log in using familiar OAuth credentials, with secure embedded wallets created quietly in the background via thirdweb.
* **Gasless Execution**: Filosign sponsors the gas internally for document workflows, so signing feels entirely free and familiar.
* **Merchant of Record Billing**: To charge for our subscription plans, we integrated Dodo Payments as our Merchant of Record. This allows teams to pay for their organization plans using standard credit cards, while their document workflows execute natively on-chain.
