# Startup outreach playbook

Execution detail for founder-led outreach. Sales philosophy, templates, and 30-day plan: [`playbook.md`](playbook.md).

I searched and compiled the practical advice. The core pattern is simple:

**Your first customers will not come from “marketing.” They will come from founder-led, painfully specific outreach to people who already feel the problem.**

For Filosign, that means: do not start with “blockchain e-signatures.” Start with people who already sign agreements and pay contributors, grantees, contractors, vendors, or partners.

**The Big Lessons**

1. **Manually recruit users first**
Paul Graham’s “Do Things That Don’t Scale” is still the best early-customer advice. Founders should manually recruit users, onboard them by hand, and do unscalable work until they understand the real buying trigger. Source: [Paul Graham](https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html?viewfullsite=1)

For Filosign: personally email 100 highly relevant teams. Offer to set up their first signing-plus-payout workflow yourself.

2. **Use your unfair network first**
Lenny’s research on early B2B startups found that many first customers came from personal networks, niche communities, direct cold emails, and investor/ecosystem networks. Stripe, Figma, Airtable, Plaid, Gusto, etc. all started by hunting for people in the exact right circles. Source: [Lenny’s Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-todays-fastest-growing-b2b-businesses)

For Filosign: your unfair network is Filecoin, FIL-B, FilOz, Akindo, Devconnect, Loops Hacker House, Web3 grant programs, and Simon/Alliance.

3. **Founder-led sales beats outsourced lead gen early**
YC’s startup sales advice is blunt: founders should do early sales because the learning matters more than the conversion. You are not just selling; you are debugging the ICP, pain, positioning, pricing, objections, and product roadmap. Source: [YC Startup School summary](https://celaeno.cloud/m/yc/startup-school/how-to-get-your-first-customers)

For Filosign: do not hand this entirely to an agent. Let the agent research leads, but you should write or approve the first 50-100 outbound messages.

4. **Relevance beats personalization**
Bad personalization is: “I saw you went to ETHDenver.”  
Good relevance is: “You run a grant program, pay contributors globally, and likely need signed milestone docs before payouts.”

For Filosign, personalize around a real operational trigger:
- grant program
- bounty program
- contractor hiring
- DAO treasury payments
- ecosystem fund
- stablecoin payment use
- global contributor network
- compliance/forms before payout

5. **The best cold emails are not “sales emails”**
They read like a founder noticed a specific workflow and is asking if it is painful.

Bad:
“Filosign is a revolutionary blockchain-powered e-signature platform.”

Good:
“I noticed you run grants at [Protocol]. How do you currently handle the signed milestone document before releasing USDC?”

6. **Don’t sell the product first. Sell the pain**
People do not buy “encrypted signatures.” They buy:
- fewer manual payout steps
- less legal/payment chaos
- cleaner audit trail
- faster contractor/grantee payments
- fewer multisig/payment coordination delays
- less risk around “who approved what?”

For Filosign, the sales wedge is:

**“You already sign agreements and pay people separately. We combine both into one workflow.”**

**What Works**

**1. Narrow ICP**
Pick one segment for the first campaign. Not “Web3 companies.”

Better segments:
- Web3 grant programs
- DAO contributor programs
- crypto foundations
- hackathon/bounty platforms
- stablecoin payroll/contractor platforms
- Filecoin/PL ecosystem companies

Start with one. I’d start with:

**Web3 grant and ecosystem teams that pay contributors in stablecoins.**

**2. Trigger-based lead lists**
Do not scrape random crypto companies. Build lists from trigger events.

Find companies that recently:
- launched a grant program
- announced an ecosystem fund
- opened RFPs
- paid retroactive grants
- hired ecosystem/contributor roles
- ran a hackathon
- announced stablecoin payments
- posted DAO treasury proposals

**3. Small batches**
Send 20-30 emails per segment. Learn. Rewrite. Repeat.

Do not send 1,000 emails with weak positioning.

**4. Simple ask**
The CTA should not be “book a demo” at first.

Better:
- “How do you handle this today?”
- “Worth comparing notes?”
- “Can I ask how you currently release payouts after milestone docs are signed?”
- “Would this be useful for one grant or contractor workflow?”

**5. Offer a pilot, not software**
Early customers do not want another tool to configure. They want a problem solved.

Offer:
“Send me one real workflow. I’ll help set it up manually and we’ll run it as a one-month pilot.”

**6. Founder credibility**
Use your proof, but briefly:
- #1 Filecoin Alpha Cohort
- $6k USDC support
- 4,000 FIL RetroPGF
- Devconnect DePIN Day showcase

Do not make the whole email about awards. Use one sentence.

**What Does Not Work**

1. **Generic cold email**
“Hi, we help companies streamline document signing” is dead.

2. **Overexplaining crypto**
Do not lead with post-quantum, Filecoin, FVM, Merkle proofs, smart contracts, or decentralized storage.

3. **Pitching everyone**
If you pitch Web2 legal teams, Web3 DAOs, freelancers, enterprises, and API developers all at once, nobody feels like it is built for them.

4. **Fake personalization**
“Loved your website” is worse than no personalization.

5. **Asking for 30 minutes too early**
If the prospect is cold, ask a sharp question first. Once they reply, then move to a call.

6. **Outsourcing discovery**
Agents can find leads. They cannot yet replace founder-level judgment on why someone should care.

**Unorthodox Growth Lessons From Cluely / Friend**

Cluely and Friend are useful case studies, but not models to copy directly.

Cluely used controversy and “ragebait” to get attention. It became widely discussed because the positioning was provocative, not polite. Source: [TechCrunch on Cluely](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/29/cluelys-roy-lee-on-the-ragebait-strategy-for-startup-marketing/)

Friend spent heavily on a provocative public campaign and turned backlash into attention. Source: [ContentGrip on Friend’s subway campaign](https://www.contentgrip.com/friend-nyc-subway-campaign/)

The lesson is not “be unethical” or “ragebait everything.” For Filosign, trust matters. You are dealing with legal documents and payments.

The usable lesson is:

**Have a sharp enemy.**

For Cluely, the enemy was fake productivity/interview norms.  
For Friend, the enemy was loneliness/social disconnection.  
For Filosign, the enemy is:

**Agreements that stop at a PDF while payment still happens manually.**

Or:

**DocuSign proves a click. It does not execute the business outcome.**

Unorthodox but safe angles for Filosign:
- Publicly roast broken Web3 payout workflows.
- Publish teardown threads: “How DAO grant payouts actually happen today.”
- Build a “Grant Payout Delay Calculator.”
- Create a public “Web3 Ops Hall of Shame”: not naming companies negatively, but mapping all the manual steps teams suffer through.
- Offer to manually migrate one grant workflow for free.
- Create “sign-to-settle” demos using real public grant templates.
- Post side-by-side flows: “Current: 11 steps. Filosign: 3 steps.”
- Turn the Sarah/W-8BEN story into a short founder post.

Do not do “ragebait.” Do **contrarian clarity**.

**How To Sell Filosign**

The sales structure:

1. **Identify the exact workflow**
Do not ask: “Do you need e-signatures?”
Ask: “What has to happen before you release a contributor or grantee payment?”

2. **Find the painful delay**
Examples:
- waiting for W-8BEN
- waiting for signed milestone acceptance
- waiting for multisig approval
- reconciling wallet payment with agreement
- proving later who approved the payout
- manual invoice chasing

3. **Make the current process feel expensive**
Not in dollars only. In time, risk, and trust.

Example:
“Right now, your agreement, approval, and payment are three separate records. That makes every payout slower and harder to audit.”

4. **Show the new world**
“With Filosign, the signed document becomes the payment condition.”

5. **Ask for one workflow**
“Can we test this on one low-risk grant or contractor payout?”

6. **Concierge the pilot**
You personally help them:
- choose document
- set recipients
- configure payout condition
- run testnet/mainnet workflow
- collect feedback
- ask what would make it production-ready

**30-Day Customer Plan For Filosign**

**Week 1: Build lead list**
Create 100 leads:
- 40 Web3 grant programs
- 25 ecosystem/devrel teams
- 20 crypto startups hiring contractors
- 15 bounty/hackathon platforms

Each lead must have:
- company
- contact name
- role
- email / Twitter / LinkedIn
- trigger signal
- suspected workflow
- personalized opener
- source URL

**Week 2: Send 30 high-quality founder emails**
Do not blast all 100. Send 30. Track replies.

Goal: 5-8 replies, 3-5 calls.

**Week 3: Run discovery calls**
Do not demo first. Ask:
- How do you pay grantees/contractors today?
- What document/form must be signed first?
- Who approves the payment?
- Where does the payment happen?
- What breaks or gets delayed?
- What would make this safe enough to use?

**Week 4: Convert 1-2 pilots**
Offer:
“One workflow, one month, white-glove setup, no charge.”

Success metric:
Not “they liked it.”  
Success is: they give you a real document/payment workflow and agree to test it.

**The Practical Philosophy**

Early sales is not persuasion. It is diagnosis.

Your job is not to convince random people that Filosign is cool. Your job is to find people already suffering from signing-plus-payment friction and show them that you understand the workflow better than anyone else.

For Filosign, the winning line is:

**“You already sign the agreement and pay separately. Filosign makes the signed agreement executable.”**
